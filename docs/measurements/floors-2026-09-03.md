## Purpose

Project G grows crustygen's vocabulary one construct at a time and re-measures the corpus after
each. The predecessor named this one from its own blocker table: linedef specials **23** and
**38**, the `lowerFloorToLowest` pair, blocked 386 and 309 of 1,282 unique maps (30.1 % and
24.1 %) and stood first and fifth in the ranking. This document records what the floor-action
construct — the drop wall, the reveal (closet and pedestal) and the bridge, through IR, compiler,
playability rules P28–P30, verifier check V-P28 and the `lift::floor` recognizer — actually
bought, and reconciles the shipped recognizer against the throwaway probe that motivated its
design.

Two numbers are reported for the conjunction, and the difference between them is the point:

- the **naïve** figure counts a map expressible when its line specials, sector specials, thing
  types, teleport lines and platforms all pass — the five-axis test the predecessor ran, now with
  the four floor specials 23/38/18/119 inside the line set;
- the **honest** figure additionally requires every floor target the map's floor lines name to be
  one of the three shapes the recognizer can state. Adding the floor specials to the vocabulary
  without that gate would claim maps whose floor actions crustygen cannot express.

driving a ceiling, a light or a door — and `neighbors_mover` (594) is the price of rule P30's
load-time destination.

### Map-level refusals, reason by reason

Both tools report, over the same 788 floor-carrying maps, the first applicable refusal reason per
map. The probe's gate **G** is the one the design argued for: no tag-0 or dangling floor line, no
gun line, and every target a *candidate* (one family, a resolved destination, an opening
sub-shape — `DropWall`, `LedgeLower`, `Bridge` with a rider who keeps their footing, or `Reveal` —
no non-floor special on its tag, and a trigger some sector can fire). Ranked in the probe's own
order, with the shipped recognizer's per-map first reason computed from the sweep's `--json`:

Every reason the two tools share matches to the map. The shipped recognizer refuses **27 more
maps**, and each of the three divergences is a gate it added on purpose:

1. **The chain rule (25 maps).** Rule P30 refuses a target whose neighbor is itself a floor
   target, a lift platform or a door sector, because both destination searches read the *current*
   floors of the neighbors: with a chain, the destination is a function of when the trigger is
   pulled, and crustygen evaluates it at load. Gate G applied no such rule; the probe priced it
   separately as its `G + no chain` row.
2. **The unsupported shape (1 map).** Gate G accepted `LedgeLower` — a ledge that lowers to join
   what is already around it — as a candidate. No IR construct states it (17 targets corpus-wide),
   so the recognizer refuses it and `V-P28` reports it by name.
3. **The activator model (1 map).** `check::plats::activator_sides`, which the recognizer shares
   with the verifier's flood and its V-P5/V-P28 invariants, is stricter than the probe's on
   walkovers in two documented ways: an `ML_BLOCKING` boundary fires from neither side, and
   neither does one with a dead-end pocket no deeper than the player's radius behind it. One map's
   last activator is one of those.

### From gate G to the shipped figure, by arithmetic

The probe's own "naïve — every non-gun floor special accepted" row is 128 all-axes maps, and the
sweep re-derives that same population independently (maps clear on sectors, things, teleports and
lifts whose only non-emittable line specials are floor specials): **128**. Every number below is a
subset of it.

| Step | Maps | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| naïve — every non-gun floor special accepted | 128 | the ceiling floors alone can reach |

| − 2 refused by the chain rule (P30) | 120 | `neighbors_mover`; gate G's `G + no chain` row prices it at −1 on its own population |

So **the after-measurement lands below the probe's 122, by design and by exactly two gates plus

reaches this population, and it costs two.

## The probe's gun-line correction

The probe's `activator_sides` treated a **gun** line (G1: specials 24 and 47) as front-side only,
on the reasoning that a hitscan reaches a line from the side its face is drawn on. The engine has
no such gate. `P_ShootSpecialLine (mobj_t* thing, line_t* line)` (pinned `p_spec.c:955-1000`) takes
**no `side` argument** and gates on none, and its only caller passes none: `PTR_ShootTraverse`
runs `if (li->special) P_ShootSpecialLine (shootthing, li);` (`p_map.c:919-920`) two lines before
it has so much as read `ML_TWOSIDED` (`p_map.c:922`). A shot from either sector the line borders
fires it. The shipped side had already been corrected — `FloorForm::shot()` (`src/tables.rs`) and
`check::plats::Dispatch::Shot` — so the probe was the last place carrying the wrong fact, and
`examples/liftprobe/common.rs` now mirrors the same three-way `Dispatch { Cross, Use, Shot }`.

The probe was re-run on this tree with the correction, over the same sample. **The design does not
move**, because gate G refuses a gun line before it ever asks who can fire it (42 map-level
refusals, unchanged). Two `§E` rows do:

Twenty triggers gain a nearer activator sector (20 move into the 0-hop bucket, out of 1, 2 and

## What this fixes for the roadmap

The line axis remains the binding constraint on the conjunction, and the geometry axes now bracket
it from below: floors 51.9 %, lifts 61.1 %, teleports 80.8 %. Of the 4,449 target refusals,
`neutral` alone is 1,382 and no vocabulary growth reaches it — a floor that changes nothing about
where the player can walk is not an action the IR is missing.

Two roadmap inputs come out of this run:

1. **More line specials, and not floor ones.** The blocker table's head — 109, 103, 48, 31, 2,
   117 — holds no floor special at all, and the pair that stood first and fifth before this
   construct has left it. Which family those six belong to is the next shape probe's question, and
   answering it that way round (measure the family, then design the construct) is how every
   construct in Project G has been chosen.
2. **The multi-sector tag, again.** 3,409 shared-tag members were *accepted* here — the floor
   recognizer takes a shared tag when every member qualifies on its own, where `lift::plat`
   refuses one outright (923 platform refusals). The two recognizers now disagree about the same
   corpus feature, which is a question for whichever construct next needs one trigger to drive
   several sectors.

## Caveats

- **Numbers are reported, never tuned.** The probe's gate G predicted 122; 119 is what the shipped
  gates deliver, and the three-map difference is decomposed above rather than closed by relaxing a
  gate.
- **Refusal rows are not row-comparable across the two tools** below the map level: the shipped
  recognizer's twelve classes and the probe's ten sit in different precedence orders and count
  targets versus maps. The map-level first-reason table above is the comparable form, and it is
  the one reported.
- **Two denominators for the shape shares.** The report's refusal rows are shares of all 9,443
  resolved targets; the probe's §D shape tables are over the targets that *have* an opening shape
  (DropWall 2,544, Reveal 3,157, Bridge 236, LedgeLower 17). The recognizer's accepted counts
  (2,172 / 2,656 / 166) are strictly smaller than the probe's, because acceptance also requires no
  conflict, one family, an activator and no chain.
- **The chain rule is measured, not modeled.** `neighbors_mover` counts a target whose neighbor
  moves at all; it does not ask whether the chain would actually change the destination. The
  probe's §G prices the tighter question — a *destination-defining* neighbor that is movable — at
  4.3 % of targets against `borders_mover`'s wider reach.
- **`raiseToTexture` is unresolved, not refused on shape.** 23 targets carry a destination this
  resolution does not load texture heights for; they are counted as `unresolved`, which is a
  measurement limit rather than a statement about the map.
- **UDMF/ZDoom-numbered floors are unmeasured** — 66 of the 1,282 sample maps are UDMF-origin and
  read Doom-numbered specials only. **Sector extent is a bounding box.** Both caveats carry over
  verbatim from the teleport measurement.
- **The conjunction curve's population changed again.** The floor axis has joined the axes that
  define it, so its checkpoints are not comparable with any earlier run's.
- **"Unloadable" is still overcounted.** Ingest runs crustywad's *strict* assembly, so maps a
  lenient assembler would load never reach the classifier. crustygen
  [#34](https://github.com/masriamir/crustygen/issues/34) tracks it; closing it adds maps to the
  denominator, so these shares are not comparable with a post-#34 run.
