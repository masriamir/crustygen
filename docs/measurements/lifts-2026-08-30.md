## Revision (2026-08-30, walkover pocket gate)

After these figures were first taken (at `e88fbb3`), commit `ef64816` narrowed
`check::plats::triggers_for`: **a walkover trigger is credited only when neither side of its line
is a dead-end pocket no deeper than the player's radius** (`dead_end_pocket`). The module's own
doc gives the engine argument — `P_TryMove` fires a walkover from its `spechit` walk, which asks
whether the thing's *center* changed sides (`P_PointOnLineSide (thing->x, thing->y, ld)`), but it
refuses the move first at `tmfloorz - thing->z > 24*FRACUNIT`, and `PIT_CheckLine` has by then
raised `tmfloorz` for every line the moving **box** straddles, `P_BoxOnLineSide` (`p_maputl.c`)
counting a box edge that merely touches a line as straddling it. So a pocket that shallow admits
no center at all: nobody crosses into it, and nobody stands in it to cross out. Both halves of the
predicate are required — shallow **and** closed on every other boundary — because §G3 of the probe
found thin *open* trigger strips 16 deep that play perfectly.

The gate can only *remove* activators, so it can only reduce `Low` walkover activators and push
platforms toward the `top_only` refusal. **The whole measurement was re-run on this tree at
`794c500` — both populations, both tools — and not one figure moved.** The regenerated
`crustygen-corpus` report is byte-for-byte identical to the one reproduced at the end of this
document, so every table above stands as printed:

## Purpose

Project G grows crustygen's vocabulary one construct at a time and re-measures the corpus after
each. The predecessor named this one from its own blocker table: linedef special **62** blocked
616 of 1,282 unique maps (48.0 %), more than any other value once 97 became emittable, and 62
and 88 are the pair the corpus spends on moving floors. This document records what the lift
construct — IR platforms, a compiler pass, playability rules, verifier checks and a
recognizer — actually bought, and reconciles the shipped recognizer against the throwaway
probe that motivated its design.

Two numbers are reported for the conjunction, and the difference between them is the point:

- the **naïve** figure counts a map expressible when its line specials, sector specials, thing
  types and teleport lines all pass — the four-axis test the predecessor ran, now with the four
  repeatable lift specials 62/88/120/123 inside the line set;
- the **honest** figure additionally requires every platform the map's lift lines name to be one
  the recognizer can state. Adding the lift specials to the vocabulary without that gate would
  claim maps whose platforms crustygen cannot express.

## The recognizer against the probe — the sample

The probe's committed tool was re-run on this tree over the same sample, so both sides of the
comparison are measurements taken today rather than transcribed tables. **Seven arbiters
reproduce exactly:**

| Arbiter | `liftprobe` | `crustygen-corpus` |
|---|---:|---:|
| maps with ≥ 1 lift line | 795 | **795** |
| platforms (unique tagged sectors) | 3,876 | **3,876** |
| platforms that cannot move | 244 | **244** |
| lift lines naming no sector (229 tag-0 + 75 unresolved) | 304 | **304** |
| maps carrying such a line | 42 | **42** |
| line axis with 62/88/120/123 emittable | 173 (13.5 %) | **173 (13.5 %)** |
| four-axis conjunction, lift shapes ignored | 133 (10.4 %) | **133 (10.4 %)** |

The shape counts do **not** match line-for-line, and that is expected: the recognizer refuses
`SharedTag` and `OneWayBarrier`, two gates the probe's `clean` test never had. The arithmetic
that closes the gap comes from the probe's second pass (`liftprobe shapes`), whose per-shape row
"sectors sharing this plat's tag" splits each shape by tag-group size:

| Shape | Probe total | Tag unshared | Shared by 2 | Shared by 3+ | Recognizer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Core → `Lift` | 1,808 | 1,498 | 113 | 197 | **1,498** |
| Pedestal | 498 | 364 | 58 | 76 | **364** |
| Barrier | 322 | 230 | 43 | 49 | **137** |
| **total** | **2,628** | **2,092** | **214** | **322** | **1,999** |

- **Core → lift, exact.** 1,808 − (113 + 197) = **1,498**, the recognizer's lift count to the
  unit. Every Core platform with an unshared tag is a recognizer `Lift`; every shared-tag one is
  refused `SharedTag`.
- **Pedestal, exact.** 498 − (58 + 76) = **364**, again to the unit.
- **Barrier, exact in two steps.** 322 − (43 + 49) = 230 unshared, then 230 − 93 = **137**; the
  93 are `OneWayBarrier` refusals — above-all platforms with two or more neighbors where not every
  neighbor can call them down.
- **Total.** 2,628 − 310 − 134 − 92 − 93 = **1,999**, the recognizer's accepted-shape total.

This decomposition is airtight rather than merely consistent, and the reason is the refusal
precedence. A probe *shape* is by definition callable from a low activator, all-repeatable,
single-speed, free of other tagged actions, and resting `Top` or `AboveAll` at one floor — which
rules out six of the recognizer's eight refusals outright. Only `SharedTag` and `OneWayBarrier`
can apply to it, so no other explanation is available for a shape the recognizer drops.

Two refusal totals exceed what the shapes account for, and both are explained by the same
precedence:

- `shared_tag` = 923, against 536 shared-tag probe shapes (310 + 134 + 92) plus 549 shared-tag
  probe `Other`s (184 + 365, from the same row on the shapes pass's `Other` bucket — 1,248 plats, the 244 dead included, not §K's 1,004 moving Others) = 1,085. The
  difference, **162**, is shared-tag platforms that cannot move: `Dead` is precedence 1 and
  `SharedTag` precedence 2, so those report `Dead` instead. 162 ≤ 244, the corpus's dead-platform
  count, as it must be. Neither tool prints that cross-tab as a row, so 162 is a residual of the
  identity, bounded but not separately measured.
- `one_way_barrier` = 104, against the 93 taken from probe Barriers. The remaining **11** are
  platforms the probe bucketed as `Other`. `OneWayBarrier` is precedence 7 and only
  `ConflictingAction` follows it, so those 11 are one-way above-all platforms that also carry a
  conflicting tagged action — the probe's "conflicting tagged action" row (168) against the
  recognizer's `conflicting` (64). The class is forced by the precedence; the count is 11 by
  subtraction.

**The individual refusal rows are not row-comparable between the two tools**, and no attempt is
made here to align them. The probe assigns one reason per `Other` platform in *its* order (not
callable from low → one-shot → mixed speed → conflicting → rest); the recognizer assigns one
refusal in a different order (dead → shared tag → one-shot → mixed speed → rest → top-only →
one-way barrier → conflicting). A platform wrong in two ways lands in different buckets on the
two sides by construction. The reconciliation above is therefore done where the two tools do
agree on criteria — the shapes — and the two extra gates.

## What this fixes for the roadmap

The line axis remains the binding constraint on the conjunction, but it is no longer the only
geometry gate that bites: the lift axis passes 61.1 % of maps — third-lowest of the five, a
third of a point above the sector-special axis's 60.8 % — and 499 of the 795 lift-carrying maps
fail it. Of the 1,877 platform refusals, `shared_tag` alone is 923, against 710 for the six
classes a richer IR could plausibly address (the seventh, `dead`, is 244 platforms that no
vocabulary growth can rescue — they do not move).

That gives two distinct roadmap inputs:

1. **More line specials.** The blocker table now opens with the floor family — 23 and 38, the
   `lowerFloorToLowest` pair, in 386 and 309 maps — which the probe's §I already identified as a
   separate mechanism from the plat: a sector that starts low and rises, or one that lowers and
   stays. That is sub-project 4's territory.
2. **A multi-sector platform.** 923 refusals, 328 tag groups, of which only 25 are the
   "one platform split by trim" case. Letting one IR trigger name several platforms is the single
   largest recognizer-side gain available on this axis, and it is a vocabulary question the line
   specials cannot answer.

## Caveats

- **Numbers are reported, never tuned.** The probe's predicted 9.4 % was an upper bound; 8.9 %
  is what the shipped gates deliver, and the six-map difference is decomposed above rather than
  closed by relaxing a gate.
- **Shape shares have two denominators.** The probe's §K shares are over 3,632 *moving* plats;
  the report's refusal rows are over all 3,876 resolved platforms. The 244 dead platforms sit
  between them, so one and the same count reads 6.7 % higher as a share of moving plats than as
  a share of all platforms.
- **The `162` and the `11` are residuals, not measured rows.** Both are forced by the refusal
  precedence and bounded by counts that were measured (244 dead platforms; 168 probe conflicting
  platforms), but neither tool emits the cross-tab that would confirm them directly. Settling
  them needs a joint per-platform dump, which is a change to a tool rather than a re-run.
- **Refusal rows are not row-comparable across the two tools**, for the precedence reason given
  above. Only the shape partition and the totals compare.
- **Sector extent is a bounding box** and **UDMF/ZDoom-numbered platforms are unmeasured** — 66
  of the 1,282 sample maps are UDMF-origin and read Doom-numbered specials only. Both caveats
  carry over verbatim from the teleport measurement; all 68 retail maps are binary Doom format,
  so the retail columns are unaffected.
- **The conjunction curve's population changed again.** The lift axis has joined the axes that
  define it, so its checkpoints are not comparable with either 2026-08-28 run's.
- **"Unloadable" is still overcounted.** Ingest runs crustywad's *strict* assembly, so maps a
  lenient assembler would load never reach the classifier. crustygen
  [#34](https://github.com/masriamir/crustygen/issues/34) tracks it; closing it adds maps to the
  denominator, so these shares are not comparable with a post-#34 run.

## Method — the exact commands

From the crustygen checkout at `794c500`, with the sample already fetched in the crustywad
checkout (`just harvest-sample 20260828 400`). Below, `$SAMPLE` is that checkout's
`xtask/data/samples/20260828-400`, `$RETAIL` its `RETAIL/`, and `$SCRATCH` a throwaway
directory outside both repositories:

```console
$ just corpus "$SAMPLE"
```
